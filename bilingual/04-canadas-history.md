## Aboriginal Peoples / 原住民

> When Europeans explored Canada they found all regions occupied by native peoples they called Indians, because the first explorers thought they had reached the East Indies. The native people lived off the land, some by hunting and gathering, others by raising crops. The Huron-Wendat of the Great Lakes region, like the Iroquois, were farmers and hunters. The Cree and Dene of the Northwest were hunter-gatherers. The Sioux were nomadic, following the bison (buffalo) herd. The Inuit lived off Arctic wildlife. West Coast natives preserved fish by drying and smoking. Warfare was common among Aboriginal groups as they competed for land, resources and prestige.

當歐洲人探勘加拿大時，發現所有地區都已有原住民居住，最初被誤稱為 **「印第安人 (Indians)」**（因為最早的探險家以為他們抵達了東印度群島）。原住民依土地維生——有人 **狩獵採集**，有人 **耕作**：

- **五大湖區的 Huron-Wendat** 與 **易洛魁 Iroquois**：農耕並狩獵
- **西北地區的 Cree 與 Dene**：狩獵採集
- **Sioux**：游牧，逐 **野牛 (bison)** 而居
- **Inuit**：以北極野生動物為生
- **西海岸原住民**：以 **乾燥與燻製** 保存魚類

各族群之間 **常為了土地、資源與聲望而戰爭**。

> The arrival of European traders, missionaries, soldiers and colonists changed the native way of life forever. Large numbers of Aboriginals died of European diseases to which they lacked immunity. However, Aboriginals and Europeans formed strong economic, religious and military bonds in the first 200 years of coexistence which laid the foundations of Canada.

歐洲商人、傳教士、士兵與殖民者的抵達 **永遠改變了原住民的生活方式**。**大量原住民死於缺乏免疫力的歐洲疾病**。然而在共存的前 200 年裡，**原住民與歐洲人在經濟、宗教與軍事上建立了強而有力的連結**，奠定了加拿大的基礎。

## The First Europeans / 最早的歐洲人

> The Vikings from Iceland who colonized Greenland 1,000 years ago also reached Labrador and the island of Newfoundland. The remains of their settlement, l'Anse aux Meadows, are a World Heritage site.

**1,000 年前** 來自冰島並殖民格陵蘭的 **維京人 (Vikings)** 也到達了 **拉布拉多與紐芬蘭島**。他們的聚落遺跡 **L'Anse aux Meadows** 現為 **世界文化遺產**。

> European exploration began in earnest in 1497 with the expedition of John Cabot, who was the first to draw a map of Canada's East Coast.

歐洲探索 **正式始於 1497 年** 義裔英國探險家 **John Cabot** 的航行——他是 **第一個繪製加拿大東岸地圖的人**。

---

## Exploring a River, Naming Canada / 探勘聖羅倫斯河、命名加拿大

> Between 1534 and 1542, Jacques Cartier made three voyages across the Atlantic, claiming the land for King Francis I of France. Cartier heard two captured guides speak the Iroquoian word **kanata**, meaning "village." By the 1550s, the name of Canada began appearing on maps.

**1534 至 1542 年間**，**Jacques Cartier** 三度橫越大西洋，**為法王法蘭索瓦一世 (Francis I)** 宣稱土地。Cartier 從兩位被俘的嚮導口中聽到易洛魁語的 **「kanata」**，意為 **「村莊」**。**1550 年代起，「Canada」之名開始出現在地圖上**。

---

## Royal New France / 新法蘭西

> In 1604, the first European settlement north of Florida was established by French explorers Pierre de Monts and Samuel de Champlain, first on St. Croix Island (in present-day Maine), then at Port-Royal, in Acadia (present-day Nova Scotia). In 1608 Champlain built a fortress at what is now Québec City.

**1604 年**，法國探險家 **Pierre de Monts** 與 **Samuel de Champlain** 建立了 **佛羅里達以北最早的歐洲聚落**——先在 **St. Croix Island**（今美國緬因州），後遷至 **Port-Royal**（阿卡迪亞，即今新斯科細亞）。**1608 年，Champlain 在今魁北克市興建堡壘**。

> The colonists struggled against a harsh climate. Champlain allied the colony with the Algonquin, Montagnais and Huron, historic enemies of the Iroquois, a confederation of five (later six) First Nations who battled with the French settlements for a century. The French and the Iroquois made peace in 1701.

殖民者面對嚴酷氣候奮鬥求生。**Champlain 與 Algonquin、Montagnais、Huron 結盟**——這些族群是 **易洛魁聯盟 (Iroquois)** 的歷史宿敵。易洛魁是由 **5（後為 6）個第一民族組成的聯盟**，與法國殖民地交戰了一個世紀。**法、易雙方於 1701 年締結和平**。

> The French and Aboriginal people collaborated in the vast fur-trade economy, driven by the demand for beaver pelts in Europe. Outstanding leaders like Jean Talon, Bishop Laval, and Count Frontenac built a French Empire in North America that reached from Hudson Bay to the Gulf of Mexico.

法國人與原住民合作經營龐大的 **毛皮貿易經濟**——以歐洲對 **河狸皮 (beaver pelts)** 的需求為驅動。**Jean Talon、Bishop Laval、Count Frontenac** 等傑出領袖在北美建立了一個 **從哈德遜灣延伸到墨西哥灣** 的法國帝國。

---

## Struggle for a Continent / 爭奪大陸

> In 1670, King Charles II of England granted the Hudson's Bay Company exclusive trading rights over the watershed draining into Hudson Bay. For the next 100 years the Company competed with Montreal-based traders. The skilled and courageous men who travelled by canoe were called voyageurs and coureurs des bois, and formed strong alliances with First Nations.

**1670 年，英王查理二世 (Charles II)** 授予 **哈德遜灣公司 (Hudson's Bay Company)** 對 **哈德遜灣集水區的獨家貿易權**。此後 100 年間，該公司與蒙特婁的商人競爭。乘獨木舟旅行的勇敢人們被稱為 **voyageurs 與 coureurs des bois**，他們與第一民族建立了強大同盟。

> English colonies along the Atlantic seaboard, dating from the early 1600s, eventually became richer and more populous than New France. In the 1700s France and Great Britain battled for control of North America. In 1759, the British defeated the French in the Battle of the Plains of Abraham at Québec City — marking the end of France's empire in America. The commanders of both armies, Brigadier James Wolfe and the Marquis de Montcalm, were killed leading their troops in battle.

**1600 年代初** 沿大西洋海岸的英國殖民地，最終變得 **比新法蘭西更富裕、更人多**。**1700 年代** 法國與英國爭奪北美的控制權。**1759 年，英軍在魁北克市的「亞伯拉罕平原戰役 (Battle of the Plains of Abraham)」擊敗法軍**——**法國在美洲的帝國從此結束**。雙方主將 **英軍 Brigadier James Wolfe** 與 **法軍 Marquis de Montcalm** **皆於戰鬥中陣亡**。

---

## The Province of Quebec / 魁北克省

> Following the war, Great Britain renamed the colony the "Province of Quebec." The French-speaking Catholic people, known as **habitants** or **Canadiens**, strove to preserve their way of life in the English-speaking, Protestant-ruled British Empire.

戰後英國將殖民地改名為 **「魁北克省 (Province of Quebec)」**。法語天主教居民——稱為 **habitants** 或 **Canadiens**——在 **英語、新教為主的大英帝國** 中努力保存自己的生活方式。

---

## A Tradition of Accommodation / 包容的傳統

> To better govern the French Roman Catholic majority, the British Parliament passed the Quebec Act of 1774. One of the constitutional foundations of Canada, the Quebec Act accommodated the principles of British institutions to the reality of the province. It allowed religious freedom for Catholics and permitted them to hold public office, a practice not then allowed in Britain. The Quebec Act restored French civil law while maintaining British criminal law.

為了治理人數佔多數的法裔天主教徒，英國國會於 **1774 年通過《魁北克法案 (Quebec Act)》**。這是 **加拿大憲政基礎之一**——它使英國制度的原則 **配合該省的現實**：

- **允許天主教徒享有宗教自由**
- **允許天主教徒擔任公職**（在當時英國本土尚不允許）
- **恢復法國民法 (civil law)**，同時 **保留英國刑法 (criminal law)**

---

## United Empire Loyalists / 保皇黨

> In 1776, the 13 British colonies to the south of Quebec declared independence and formed the United States. North America was again divided by war. More than 40,000 people loyal to the Crown, called "Loyalists," fled the oppression of the American Revolution to settle in Nova Scotia and Quebec. Joseph Brant led thousands of Loyalist Mohawk Indians into Canada. The Loyalists came from Dutch, German, British, Scandinavian, Aboriginal and other origins and from Presbyterian, Anglican, Baptist, Methodist, Jewish, Quaker and Catholic religious backgrounds.

**1776 年，魁北克以南的 13 個英屬殖民地宣布獨立**，組成美國。北美再度因戰爭分裂。**超過 40,000 名效忠王室的「保皇黨 (Loyalists)」** 逃避美國革命的壓迫，定居於 **新斯科細亞與魁北克**。**Joseph Brant 領數千名 Mohawk 印第安保皇黨進入加拿大**。Loyalists 包含 **荷蘭、德國、英國、北歐、原住民等族裔**，宗教上來自 **長老會、聖公會、浸信會、衛理公會、猶太教、貴格會、天主教** 等背景。

> About 3,000 black Loyalists, freedmen and slaves came north seeking a better life. In turn, in 1792, some black Nova Scotians, who were given poor land, moved on to establish Freetown, Sierra Leone (West Africa), a new British colony for freed slaves.

約 **3,000 名黑人保皇黨、獲釋黑人與奴隸** 北上尋求更好的生活。**1792 年，部分被分配到貧瘠土地的黑人新斯科細亞人** 移居西非，建立 **獅子山 (Sierra Leone) 的自由城 (Freetown)**——一個給獲釋黑奴的新英屬殖民地。

---

## The Beginnings of Democracy / 民主的萌芽

> Democratic institutions developed gradually and peacefully. The first representative assembly was elected in Halifax, Nova Scotia, in 1758. Prince Edward Island followed in 1773, New Brunswick in 1785. The Constitutional Act of 1791 divided the Province of Quebec into Upper Canada (later Ontario), which was mainly Loyalist, Protestant and English-speaking, and Lower Canada (later Quebec), heavily Catholic and French-speaking.

民主制度逐步且和平地發展。**首個代議議會於 1758 年在新斯科細亞哈利法克斯選出**。**愛德華王子島於 1773、新布朗斯維克於 1785** 跟進。**1791 年《憲法法案 (Constitutional Act)》** 將魁北克省分為：

- **上加拿大 (Upper Canada，即今 Ontario)**：以保皇黨、新教、英語為主
- **下加拿大 (Lower Canada，即今 Quebec)**：以天主教與法語為主

> The Act also granted to the Canadas, for the first time, legislative assemblies elected by the people. The name Canada also became official at this time and has been used ever since. The Atlantic colonies and the two Canadas were known collectively as British North America.

該法案 **首次賦予兩個加拿大省由人民選舉的立法議會**。**「Canada」之名自此正式使用**。大西洋殖民地與兩個加拿大省合稱 **「英屬北美 (British North America)」**。

## Abolition of Slavery / 廢除奴隸制

> Slavery has existed all over the world, from Asia, Africa and the Middle East to the Americas. The first movement to abolish the transatlantic slave trade emerged in the British Parliament in the late 1700s. In 1793, Upper Canada, led by Lieutenant Governor John Graves Simcoe, a Loyalist military officer, became the first province in the Empire to move toward abolition. In 1807, the British Parliament prohibited the buying and selling of slaves, and in 1833 abolished slavery throughout the Empire. Thousands of slaves escaped from the United States, followed "the North Star" and settled in Canada via the Underground Railroad, a Christian anti-slavery network.

奴隸制度全球皆有。廢除跨大西洋奴隸貿易的運動於 **1700 年代末** 從 **英國國會** 開始。**1793 年，上加拿大在副省督 John Graves Simcoe（保皇黨軍官）的領導下，成為大英帝國第一個朝廢奴邁進的省份**。**1807 年英國國會禁止奴隸買賣，1833 年全帝國廢除奴隸制度**。數千名美國奴隸 **沿「北極星」**，透過 **「地下鐵路 (Underground Railroad)」**——一個基督教反奴隸網絡——逃到加拿大定居。

## A Growing Economy / 經濟成長

> The first companies in Canada were formed during the French and British regimes and competed for the fur trade. The Hudson's Bay Company, with French, British and Aboriginal employees, came to dominate the trade in the northwest from Fort Garry (Winnipeg) and Fort Edmonton to Fort Langley (near Vancouver) and Fort Victoria—trading posts that later became cities.

加拿大最早的公司在法國與英國時期形成，競爭毛皮貿易。**哈德遜灣公司** 雇用法、英與原住民員工，從 **Fort Garry（Winnipeg）、Fort Edmonton 到 Fort Langley（Vancouver 附近）與 Fort Victoria** ——這些貿易據點後來都成為城市。

> The first financial institutions opened in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. The Montreal Stock Exchange opened in 1832. For centuries Canada's economy was based mainly on farming and on exporting natural resources such as fur, fish and timber, transported by roads, lakes, rivers and canals.

最早的金融機構出現在 **18 世紀末與 19 世紀初**。**蒙特婁證券交易所於 1832 年開業**。幾個世紀以來，加拿大經濟以 **農業與毛皮、漁、木材等自然資源出口** 為主，透過 **公路、湖泊、河流與運河** 運輸。

---

## The War of 1812: The Fight for Canada / 1812 年戰爭

> After the defeat of Napoleon Bonaparte's fleet in the Battle of Trafalgar (1805), the Royal Navy ruled the waves. The British Empire, which included Canada, fought to resist Bonaparte's bid to dominate Europe. This led to American resentment at British interference with their shipping. Believing it would be easy to conquer Canada, the United States launched an invasion in June 1812.

**拿破崙艦隊於 1805 年的特拉法加海戰 (Battle of Trafalgar) 戰敗後**，英國皇家海軍稱霸海洋。大英帝國（包括加拿大）對抗拿破崙的歐洲霸權。**英國干涉美國船運引發美國不滿**。**美國以為可以輕易征服加拿大，於 1812 年 6 月發動入侵**。

> The Americans were mistaken. Canadian volunteers and First Nations, including Shawnee led by Chief Tecumseh, supported British soldiers in Canada's defence. In July, Major-General Sir Isaac Brock captured Detroit but was killed while defending against an American attack at Queenston Heights, near Niagara Falls, a battle the Americans lost. In 1813, Lieutenant-Colonel Charles de Salaberry and 460 soldiers, mostly French Canadiens, turned back 4,000 American invaders at Châteauguay, south of Montreal.

但美國錯了。**加拿大志願軍與第一民族**（包括 **首領 Tecumseh 率領的 Shawnee**）支援英軍防衛加拿大。**7 月，少將 Sir Isaac Brock 攻下底特律**，但在 **尼加拉瀑布附近的 Queenston Heights** 防衛美軍進攻時 **陣亡**——此戰美軍敗北。**1813 年，中校 Charles de Salaberry 率 460 名士兵（多為法裔加拿大人），在蒙特婁南方的 Châteauguay 擊退 4,000 名美軍入侵者**。

> In 1813 the Americans burned Government House and the Parliament Buildings in York (now Toronto). In retaliation in 1814, Major-General Robert Ross led an expedition from Nova Scotia that burned down the White House and other public buildings in Washington, D.C. Ross died in battle soon afterwards and was buried in Halifax with full military honours.

**1813 年，美軍焚毀 York（今 Toronto）的政府辦公廳與國會建築**。**作為報復，1814 年少將 Robert Ross 率部隊從新斯科細亞出發，焚毀華盛頓特區的白宮與其他公共建築**。Ross 不久後戰死，於哈利法克斯以全套軍禮安葬。

> By 1814, the American attempt to conquer Canada had failed. The British paid for a costly Canadian defence system, including the Citadels at Halifax and Québec City, the naval drydock at Halifax and Fort Henry at Kingston—today popular historic sites. The present-day Canada-U.S.A. border is partly an outcome of the War of 1812, which ensured that Canada would remain independent of the United States.

**1814 年美國征服加拿大的企圖宣告失敗**。**今天的加美邊界部分即是 1812 戰爭的結果**——這場戰爭確保了加拿大不被美國併吞。

> In 1813, Laura Secord, pioneer wife and mother of five children, made a dangerous 19-mile (30-km) journey on foot to warn Lieutenant James FitzGibbon of a planned American attack. Her bravery contributed to victory at the Battle of Beaver Dams. She is recognized as a heroine to this day.

**1813 年，五個孩子的母親、開拓者妻子 Laura Secord，徒步走了 19 英里（30 公里）的危險旅程，警告 FitzGibbon 中尉美軍即將進攻**。她的勇氣促成了 **Beaver Dams 戰役** 的勝利，**她至今仍被尊為英雄**。

## Rebellions of 1837–38 / 1837–38 叛亂

> In the 1830s, reformers in Upper and Lower Canada believed that progress toward full democracy was too slow. Some believed Canada should adopt American republican values or even try to join the United States. When armed rebellions occurred in 1837–38 in the area outside Montreal and in Toronto, the rebels did not have enough public support to succeed. They were defeated by British troops and Canadian volunteers. A number of rebels were hanged or exiled; some exiles later returned to Canada.

**1830 年代**，上下加拿大的改革派認為民主進展太慢。**部分人主張加拿大應採美式共和價值，甚至加入美國**。**1837–38 年蒙特婁外圍與多倫多爆發武裝叛亂**，但叛軍 **未獲足夠民意支持**，被英軍與加拿大志願軍擊敗。部分叛亂者被處絞刑或流放，部分流放者後回到加拿大。

> Lord Durham, an English reformer sent to report on the rebellions, recommended that Upper and Lower Canada be merged and given responsible government. This meant that the ministers of the Crown must have the support of a majority of the elected representatives in order to govern. Controversially, Lord Durham also said that the quickest way for the Canadiens to achieve progress was to assimilate into English-speaking Protestant culture. This recommendation demonstrated a complete lack of understanding of French Canadians, who sought to uphold the distinct identity of French Canada.

英國改革派 **Lord Durham** 受派調查叛亂，建議 **將上下加拿大合併並給予責任政府 (responsible government)**——意即 **內閣大臣必須獲得多數民選代表支持才能執政**。但 Durham 也具爭議地主張 **法裔加拿大人最快的進步方式就是同化於英語新教文化**——這顯示他 **完全不了解法裔加拿大人** 想要維護自身獨特身分的願望。

> Some reformers, including Sir Étienne-Paschal Taché and Sir George-Étienne Cartier, later became Fathers of Confederation, as did a former member of the voluntary government militia in Upper Canada, Sir John A. Macdonald.

部分改革派，包括 **Sir Étienne-Paschal Taché 和 Sir George-Étienne Cartier**，後來成為 **邦聯之父**，**John A. Macdonald** 也是。

## Responsible Government / 責任政府

> In 1840, Upper and Lower Canada were united as the Province of Canada. Reformers such as Sir Louis-Hippolyte La Fontaine and Robert Baldwin, in parallel with Joseph Howe in Nova Scotia, worked with British governors toward responsible government.

**1840 年，上下加拿大合併為「加拿大省 (Province of Canada)」**。改革派 **Sir Louis-Hippolyte La Fontaine** 與 **Robert Baldwin**，加上新斯科細亞的 **Joseph Howe**，與英國總督合作，推動 **責任政府**。

> The first British North American colony to attain full responsible government was Nova Scotia in 1847–48. In 1848–49 the governor of United Canada, Lord Elgin, with encouragement from London, introduced responsible government.

**英屬北美最早達成完全責任政府的是 1847–48 年的新斯科細亞**。**1848–49 年，聯合加拿大總督 Lord Elgin 在倫敦的鼓勵下引入責任政府**。

> This is the system that we have today: if the government loses a confidence vote in the assembly it must resign. La Fontaine, a champion of democracy and French language rights, became the first leader of a responsible government in the Canadas.

**這就是我們今天的制度**：若政府在議會中失去信任投票，必須下台。**La Fontaine 成為加拿大首位責任政府領袖**，是民主與法語權利的捍衛者。

## Confederation / 邦聯

> From 1864 to 1867, representatives of Nova Scotia, New Brunswick and the Province of Canada, with British support, worked together to establish a new country. These men are known as the Fathers of Confederation. They created two levels of government: federal and provincial. The old Province of Canada was split into two new provinces: Ontario and Quebec, which, together with New Brunswick and Nova Scotia, formed the new country called the Dominion of Canada. Each province would elect its own legislature and have control of such areas as education and health.

**1864–1867 年間**，新斯科細亞、新布朗斯維克與加拿大省的代表在英國支持下合作建立新國家。這些人被稱為 **「邦聯之父 (Fathers of Confederation)」**。他們創建了 **聯邦與省兩級政府**。加拿大省被拆為 **Ontario 和 Quebec**，加上 **New Brunswick 與 Nova Scotia**，組成新國家 **「加拿大自治領 (Dominion of Canada)」**。每個省選舉自己的議會，負責 **教育、健保** 等領域。

> The British Parliament passed the British North America Act in 1867. The Dominion of Canada was officially born on July 1, 1867. Until 1982, July 1 was celebrated as "Dominion Day" to commemorate the day that Canada became a self-governing Dominion. Today it is officially known as Canada Day.

**英國國會於 1867 年通過《英屬北美法案 (British North America Act)》**。**加拿大自治領於 1867 年 7 月 1 日正式誕生**。直到 1982 年，7 月 1 日被稱為 **「自治領日 (Dominion Day)」**；今日正式稱為 **「加拿大日 (Canada Day)」**。

## Dominion from Sea to Sea / 從海到海的自治領

> Sir Leonard Tilley, an elected official and Father of Confederation from New Brunswick, suggested the term **Dominion of Canada** in 1864. He was inspired by Psalm 72 in the Bible which refers to "dominion from sea to sea and from the river to the ends of the earth." This phrase embodied the vision of building a powerful, united, wealthy and free country that spanned a continent.

來自新布朗斯維克的民選官員、邦聯之父 **Sir Leonard Tilley**，於 1864 年提議 **「加拿大自治領 (Dominion of Canada)」** 這個名稱。他的靈感來自聖經 **詩篇 72 篇**：「**dominion from sea to sea and from the river to the ends of the earth（從海到海，從大河延及地極的權柄）**」。這句話體現了 **建立一個橫跨大陸、強大、團結、富裕、自由國家** 的願景。

## Canada's First Prime Minister / 加拿大首任總理

> In 1867, Sir John Alexander Macdonald, a Father of Confederation, became Canada's first Prime Minister. Born in Scotland on January 11, 1815, he came to Upper Canada as a child. He was a lawyer in Kingston, Ontario, a gifted politician and a colourful personality. Parliament has recognized January 11 as Sir John A. Macdonald Day. His portrait is on the $10 bill.

**1867 年，邦聯之父 Sir John Alexander Macdonald 成為加拿大首任總理**。他 **1815 年 1 月 11 日生於蘇格蘭**，幼年隨家人移居上加拿大，後在 Ontario 京斯敦執律師業，是一位有才華且個性鮮明的政治家。**國會將 1 月 11 日定為 Sir John A. Macdonald Day**。**他的肖像曾印在 $10 紙鈔上**（注：2018 年後 $10 已改為 Viola Desmond）。

> Sir George-Étienne Cartier was the key architect of Confederation from Quebec. A railway lawyer, Montrealer, close ally of Macdonald and patriotic Canadien, Cartier led Quebec into Confederation and helped negotiate the entry of the Northwest Territories, Manitoba and British Columbia into Canada.

**Sir George-Étienne Cartier** 是來自魁北克的邦聯關鍵推手——他是鐵路律師、蒙特婁人、Macdonald 的密友、愛國的法裔加拿大人，**領 Quebec 加入邦聯**，並協助談判 **NWT、Manitoba、BC** 加入加拿大。

## Challenge in the West / 西部的挑戰

> When Canada took over the vast northwest region from the Hudson's Bay Company in 1869, the 12,000 Métis of the Red River were not consulted. In response, Louis Riel led an armed uprising and seized Fort Garry, the territorial capital. Canada's future was in jeopardy. How could the Dominion reach from sea to sea if it could not control the interior?

**1869 年，加拿大從哈德遜灣公司接管廣大的西北地區，但 Red River 的 12,000 名梅蒂斯人未被諮詢**。作為回應，**Louis Riel 領導武裝起義，奪取領地首府 Fort Garry**。加拿大的未來陷入危機——若無法控制內陸，何以「從海到海」？

> Ottawa sent soldiers to retake Fort Garry in 1870. Riel fled to the United States and Canada established a new province: Manitoba. Riel was elected to Parliament but never took his seat. Later, as Métis and Indian rights were again threatened by westward settlement, a second rebellion in 1885 in present-day Saskatchewan led to Riel's trial and execution for high treason, a decision that was strongly opposed in Quebec. Riel is seen by many as a hero, a defender of Métis rights and the father of Manitoba.

**1870 年渥太華派兵奪回 Fort Garry，Riel 逃往美國，加拿大建立新省 Manitoba**。Riel 雖當選國會議員，卻從未就職。後來，**1885 年在今 Saskatchewan 發生第二次叛亂**——Riel **被審判並以叛國罪處絞**，此決定在 Quebec 強烈反對。**Riel 被許多人視為英雄、梅蒂斯權利的捍衛者、Manitoba 之父**。

> After the first Métis uprising, Prime Minister Macdonald established the North West Mounted Police (NWMP) in 1873 to pacify the West and assist in negotiations with the Indians. The NWMP founded Fort Calgary, Fort MacLeod and other centres that today are cities and towns. Regina became its headquarters. Today, the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP or "the Mounties") are the national police force and one of Canada's best-known symbols.

第一次梅蒂斯叛亂後，**Macdonald 總理於 1873 年成立「西北騎警 (NWMP)」** 平息西部與協助與印第安人談判。NWMP 建立了 **Fort Calgary、Fort MacLeod** 等中心，後成為今日的城市。**Regina 為其總部**。今天，**皇家加拿大騎警 (RCMP, "the Mounties")** 是國家警察，也是 **加拿大最知名的象徵之一**。

## A Railway from Sea to Sea / 橫貫海岸的鐵路

> British Columbia joined Canada in 1871 after Ottawa promised to build a railway to the West Coast. On November 7, 1885, a powerful symbol of unity was completed when Donald Smith (Lord Strathcona), the Scottish-born director of the Canadian Pacific Railway (CPR), drove the last spike. The project was financed by British and American investors and built by both European and Chinese labour. Afterwards the Chinese were subject to discrimination, including the Head Tax, a race-based entry fee. The Government of Canada apologized in 2006 for this discriminatory policy.

**British Columbia 於 1871 年加入加拿大**，條件是渥太華 **承諾興建一條通往西海岸的鐵路**。**1885 年 11 月 7 日，CPR（加拿大太平洋鐵路）蘇格蘭裔董事 Donald Smith（Lord Strathcona）打下最後一根道釘**，象徵國家統一。**工程由英美投資、歐亞勞工共建**。**完工後華工受到歧視，包括以族裔為基礎的「人頭稅 (Head Tax)」**。**加拿大政府於 2006 年正式為此歧視政策道歉**。

## Moving Westward / 向西發展

> Canada's economy grew and became more industrialized during the economic boom of the 1890s and early 1900s. One million British and one million Americans immigrated to Canada at this time.

**1890 年代與 1900 年代初** 的繁榮使加拿大經濟成長與工業化。**約 100 萬英國人與 100 萬美國人** 於此期間移民加拿大。

> Sir Wilfrid Laurier became the first French-Canadian prime minister since Confederation and encouraged immigration to the West. His portrait is on the $5 bill. The railway made it possible for immigrants, including 170,000 Ukrainians, 115,000 Poles and tens of thousands from Germany, France, Norway and Sweden to settle in the West before 1914 and develop a thriving agricultural sector.

**Sir Wilfrid Laurier 成為邦聯後第一位法裔加拿大籍總理**，鼓勵移民到西部。**他的肖像印在 $5 紙鈔上**。鐵路讓 **17 萬烏克蘭人、11.5 萬波蘭人、數萬德、法、挪威、瑞典人** 在 1914 年前移居西部，發展出蓬勃的農業。

## Women Get the Vote / 婦女取得投票權

> At the time of Confederation, the vote was limited to property-owning adult white males. This was common in most democratic countries at the time. The effort by women to achieve the right to vote is known as the women's suffrage movement. Its founder in Canada was Dr. Emily Stowe, the first Canadian woman to practise medicine in Canada. In 1916, Manitoba became the first province to grant voting rights to women.

邦聯時期，**投票權僅限於擁有財產的成年白人男性**——這在當時多數民主國家很普遍。**婦女爭取投票權的運動稱為「婦女參政運動 (women's suffrage movement)」**。**加拿大運動的創始者是 Dr. Emily Stowe，加拿大第一位執業女醫師**。**1916 年，Manitoba 成為第一個給予婦女投票權的省份**。

> In 1917, thanks to the leadership of women such as Dr. Stowe and other suffragettes, the federal government of Sir Robert Borden gave women the right to vote in federal elections — first to nurses at the battle front, then to women who were related to men in active wartime service. In 1918, most Canadian female citizens aged 21 and over were granted the right to vote in federal elections. In 1921 Agnes Macphail, a farmer and teacher, became the first woman MP. Due to the work of Thérèse Casgrain and others, Quebec granted women the vote in 1940.

**1917 年**，得益於 Dr. Stowe 與其他婦女領袖，**Sir Robert Borden 政府** 賦予婦女聯邦投票權——先給前線護士，再給戰時服役男性的女眷。**1918 年，多數 21 歲以上加拿大女性公民取得聯邦投票權**。**1921 年，農民、教師 Agnes Macphail 成為首位女性 MP**。由於 **Thérèse Casgrain** 等人的努力，**Quebec 於 1940 年才賦予婦女投票權**。

## The First World War / 第一次世界大戰

> Most Canadians were proud to be part of the British Empire. Over 7,000 volunteered to fight in the South African War (1899–1902), popularly known as the Boer War, and over 260 died.

多數加拿大人 **以身為大英帝國一員為傲**。**逾 7,000 人志願參加 1899–1902 年波耳戰爭 (Boer War)，260 餘人陣亡**。

> When Germany attacked Belgium and France in 1914 and Britain declared war, Ottawa formed the Canadian Expeditionary Force (later the Canadian Corps). More than 600,000 Canadians served in the war, most of them volunteers, out of a total population of eight million.

**1914 年德國攻擊比利時與法國，英國宣戰，渥太華組建「加拿大遠征軍 (CEF)」（後稱「加拿大軍團」）**。**在 800 萬總人口中，逾 60 萬加拿大人服役**，多為志願軍。

> On the battlefield, the Canadians proved to be tough, innovative soldiers. The Canadian Corps captured Vimy Ridge in April 1917, with 10,000 killed or wounded, securing the Canadians' reputation for valour as the "shock troops of the British Empire." One Canadian officer said: "It was Canada from the Atlantic to the Pacific on parade ... In those few minutes I witnessed the birth of a nation." **April 9 is celebrated as Vimy Day**.

戰場上加拿大人證明自己 **堅強、創新**。**1917 年 4 月加拿大軍團攻下 Vimy Ridge（10,000 人傷亡）**，確立 **「大英帝國衝擊部隊」** 的英勇美譽。一位加拿大軍官說：「**那是從大西洋到太平洋的整個加拿大在閱兵……短短幾分鐘內，我見證了一個國家的誕生。**」**4 月 9 日被定為 Vimy Day**。

> Regrettably, from 1914 to 1920, Ottawa interned over 8,000 former Austro-Hungarian subjects, mainly Ukrainian men, as "enemy aliens" in 24 labour camps across Canada, even though Britain advised against the policy.

**1914–1920 年，渥太華將逾 8,000 名前奧匈帝國臣民（多為烏克蘭男性）以「敵國僑民」名義關進全國 24 個勞改營**——即使英國建議不要如此。

> In 1918, under the command of General Sir Arthur Currie, Canada's greatest soldier, the Canadian Corps advanced alongside the French and British Empire troops in the last hundred days. These included the victorious Battle of Amiens on August 8, 1918–which the Germans called "the black day of the German Army"–followed by Arras, Canal du Nord, Cambrai and Mons. With Germany and Austria's surrender, the war ended in the Armistice on November 11, 1918. In total **60,000 Canadians were killed and 170,000 wounded**.

**1918 年**，**加拿大史上最偉大的軍人 General Sir Arthur Currie** 指揮加拿大軍團與英法部隊在最後一百日進攻。包括 **1918 年 8 月 8 日勝利的阿米恩斯戰役 (Battle of Amiens)**——德國人稱之為 **「德軍的黑色之日」**——以及隨後的 Arras、Canal du Nord、Cambrai、Mons。**德奧投降，戰爭於 1918 年 11 月 11 日停戰 (Armistice) 結束**。**共有 60,000 名加拿大人陣亡、170,000 人受傷**。

## Remembrance Day / 國殤紀念日

> Canadians remember the sacrifices of our veterans and brave fallen in all wars up to the present day in which Canadians took part, each year on November 11: Remembrance Day. Canadians wear the red poppy and observe a moment of silence at the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month to honour the sacrifices of over a million brave men and women who have served, and the 110,000 who have given their lives.

每年 **11 月 11 日國殤紀念日 (Remembrance Day)**，加拿大人紀念至今所有戰爭中犧牲的退伍軍人與烈士。加拿大人 **佩戴紅罌粟花 (red poppy)**，於 **11 月 11 日 11 時** 默哀片刻，以紀念逾百萬服役者與 11 萬陣亡者的犧牲。

> Canadian medical officer Lieutenant-Colonel John McCrae composed the poem "In Flanders Fields" in 1915.

**加拿大軍醫中校 John McCrae 於 1915 年寫下《In Flanders Fields》詩篇**。

### In Flanders Fields（節選翻譯）

> In Flanders fields the poppies blow / Between the crosses, row on row, / That mark our place; and in the sky / The larks, still bravely singing, fly / Scarce heard amid the guns below.

弗蘭德斯野上罌粟花飄揚，
在我們長眠的十字架間排成行。
天上雲雀仍勇敢地歌唱，
但在底下隆隆的砲火中幾不可聞。

> We are the dead. Short days ago / We lived, felt dawn, saw sunset glow, / Loved, and were loved, and now we lie / In Flanders fields.

我們已是亡者。不久之前，
我們也曾活著，感受清晨、看見落日，
愛人，被愛，如今卻長眠
於弗蘭德斯野。

> Take up our quarrel with the foe: / To you from failing hands we throw / The torch; be yours to hold it high. / If ye break faith with us who die / We shall not sleep, though poppies grow / In Flanders fields.

接過我們與敵人的戰鬥：
我們從垂落的手中將火炬擲予你，
請你高舉。若你背棄了我們這些死者的信約，
即使罌粟花在弗蘭德斯野盛開，
我們也將不得安眠。

## Between the Wars / 戰間期

> After the First World War, the British Empire evolved into a free association of states known as the British Commonwealth of Nations. Canada remains a leading member of the Commonwealth to this day, together with other successor states of the Empire such as India, Australia, New Zealand, and several African and Caribbean countries.

一戰後，大英帝國演變為 **「大英國協 (British Commonwealth of Nations)」**——一個自由結合的國家組織。加拿大至今仍是國協的主要成員，與印度、澳洲、紐西蘭等繼承國一同。

> The "Roaring Twenties" were boom times, with prosperity for businesses and low unemployment. The stock market crash of 1929, however, led to the Great Depression or the "Dirty Thirties." Unemployment reached 27% in 1933 and many businesses were wiped out. Farmers in Western Canada were hit hardest by low grain prices and a terrible drought.

**「咆哮的二十年代 (Roaring Twenties)」是繁榮期**，企業興旺、失業率低。**1929 年股市崩盤導致大蕭條（「骯髒的三十年代」）**。**1933 年失業率達 27%**，大量企業倒閉。西部農民因穀價低與大旱受創最重。

> There was growing demand for the government to create a social safety net with minimum wages, a standard work week and programs such as unemployment insurance. The Bank of Canada, a central bank to manage the money supply and bring stability to the financial system, was created in 1934.

愈來愈多人要求政府建立 **社會安全網**：**最低工資、標準工時、失業保險**。**1934 年加拿大銀行 (Bank of Canada)——中央銀行——成立**。

## The Second World War / 第二次世界大戰

> The Second World War began in 1939 when Adolf Hitler, the National Socialist (Nazi) dictator of Germany, invaded Poland and conquered much of Europe. Canada joined with its democratic allies in the fight to defeat tyranny by force of arms.

**1939 年，德國納粹獨裁者希特勒入侵波蘭並征服大部分歐洲**，二戰爆發。加拿大與其民主盟邦一同參戰。

> More than one million Canadians and Newfoundlanders (Newfoundland was a separate British entity) served in the Second World War, out of a population of 11.5 million. This was a high proportion and of these, **44,000 were killed**.

**1,150 萬人口中，逾 100 萬加拿大人與紐芬蘭人服役**（當時 Newfoundland 尚為獨立的英屬實體）。其中 **44,000 人陣亡**。

> The Canadians fought bravely and suffered losses in the unsuccessful defence of Hong Kong (1941) from attack by Imperial Japan, and in a failed raid on Nazi-controlled Dieppe on the coast of France (1942).

加拿大人英勇奮戰，於 **1941 年香港防衛戰** 對抗日本帝國、**1942 年 Dieppe 突襲（失敗）** 中蒙受重大損失。

> The Royal Canadian Air Force (RCAF) took part in the Battle of Britain and provided a high proportion of Commonwealth aircrew in bombers and fighter planes over Europe. Moreover, Canada contributed more to the Allied air effort than any other Commonwealth country, with over 130,000 Allied air crew trained in Canada under the British Commonwealth Air Training Plan.

**皇家加拿大空軍 (RCAF) 參與不列顛戰役**，並提供大量盟邦空勤人員。**加拿大對盟軍空戰的貢獻超過任何其他國協國家**——在 **「英聯空中訓練計劃」** 下於加拿大訓練超過 130,000 名盟軍空勤。

> The Royal Canadian Navy (RCN) saw its finest hour in the Battle of the Atlantic, protecting convoys of merchant ships against German submarines. Canada's Merchant Navy helped to feed, clothe and resupply Britain. At the end of the Second World War, **Canada had the third-largest navy in the world**.

**皇家加拿大海軍 (RCN) 的巔峰時刻是大西洋戰役**——保護商船船團對抗德軍潛艇。加拿大商船隊協助餵養、補給英國。**二戰結束時，加拿大擁有世界第三大海軍**。

> In the Pacific war, Japan invaded the Aleutian Islands, attacked a lighthouse on Vancouver Island, launched fire balloons over B.C. and the Prairies, and grossly maltreated Canadian prisoners of war captured at Hong Kong. Japan surrendered on August 14, 1945—the end of four years of war in the Pacific.

太平洋戰場中，**日本入侵阿留申群島、攻擊溫哥華島燈塔、向 BC 與草原省發射火氣球**，並嚴重虐待香港被俘的加軍戰俘。**日本於 1945 年 8 月 14 日投降**——太平洋戰爭結束。

> Regrettably, the state of war and public opinion in B.C. led to the forcible relocation of Canadians of Japanese origin by the federal government and the sale of their property without compensation. This occurred even though the military and the RCMP told Ottawa that they posed little danger to Canada. **The Government of Canada apologized in 1988 for wartime wrongs and compensated the victims.**

戰爭狀態與 BC 輿論導致 **聯邦政府強制遷離日裔加拿大人，且未經補償變賣其財產**——儘管軍方與 RCMP 告知渥太華他們對加拿大幾無威脅。**加拿大政府於 1988 年正式為戰時錯誤道歉並補償受害者**。

---

## D-Day / D 日

> In order to defeat Nazism and Fascism, the Allies invaded Nazi-occupied Europe. Canadians took part in the liberation of Italy in 1943–44. In the epic invasion of Normandy in northern France on June 6, 1944, known as D-Day, 15,000 Canadian troops stormed and captured Juno Beach from the German Army. Approximately one in ten Allied soldiers on D-Day was Canadian. The Canadian Army liberated the Netherlands in 1944–45 and helped force the German surrender of May 8, 1945, bringing to an end six years of war in Europe.

為了擊敗納粹與法西斯主義，盟軍進攻納粹佔領下的歐洲。**加拿大人於 1943–44 年參與義大利的解放**。**1944 年 6 月 6 日諾曼第大登陸（D-Day），15,000 名加拿大士兵衝鋒佔領 Juno Beach**。**D-Day 當日每 10 名盟軍士兵就有 1 名是加拿大人**。**加拿大軍於 1944–45 年解放荷蘭**，並協助迫使 **1945 年 5 月 8 日德國投降**，結束了六年的歐洲戰爭。
